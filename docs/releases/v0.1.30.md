# Release v0.1.30

**A portable forge-infrastructure contract for resolving capabilities, binding
authority, and verifying live grants without wrapping a provider CLI.**

## Overview

v0.1.30 adds the draft `forge-infra/v0` contract family. It lets a consumer
describe portable requirements against git-hosting infrastructure, resolve them
against evidence-backed provider profiles, acquire authority through documented
mint/handoff paths, and verify live grants — without treating GitHub, GitLab,
and Forgejo as one SDK and without storing secrets in contract artifacts.

The family is a capability-resolution and control model. It is not a common
provider CLI, a git implementation, or a webhook delivery bus.

## Highlights

### Seven-lane model

The contract separates concerns that evolve at different rates:

- intent and resolution (requirement profiles and digest-bound binding plans)
- authority control (authority profiles and non-secret transition receipts)
- native git (hosted repository container and content *authority*)
- forge resources (issues, snippets, packages, releases)
- automation (workflow definitions, runs, runners)
- events and telemetry (subscriptions, statuses, checks)
- assurance (time-bounded verification, drift, revocation)

Native git content mutation stays git or a mapped contents API. Delivery of
webhooks stays in sibling wait/process/artifact contracts.

### Neutral catalog and curated provider profiles

A hostless capability catalog names jobs and operations without vendor product
strings. Provider profiles map native features and grant names onto those
operations with dated evidence. Support is claimed per operation and authority
class, not as a feature-level boolean.

### Bind and verify

A binding receipt records one completed authority transition. A verification
receipt reports whether live grants still match the plan, with a finite
validity interval. Policy engines consume closed facts; the contract does not
embed allow/deny.

### Reader guide

`docs/guides/using-the-forge-infrastructure-contract.md` is the mechanics
companion: information model, four coupled concerns, sequence diagrams, and
sibling-contract map. The standard remains normative.

### Executable coverage

Positive examples, structural and semantic rejects, policy-input and bind-chain
invariants, and the contract manifest participate in `make check`.

## Compatibility

`forge-infra/v0` is a new draft contract family and does not change existing
contract families. Consumers should pin the repository revision they adopt
while the family remains at `v0`.

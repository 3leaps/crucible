# Release v0.1.25

**The machinery catches up to the records: an executable run-book for the
review standard, and a review-journal contract that now enforces what it
previously documented — with every enforcement demonstrated able to fail.**

## Overview

v0.1.24 ratified the fierce-collaboration review methodology. v0.1.25 also
carries the repository's shipping charter — PDR-0006, accepted after v0.1.24
was cut — and makes the review methodology usable and checkable: a step-by-step guide a team can actually run, and an
enforcement pass over `review-journal/v0` that converts the contract's
documented rules into schema and journal-set checks, each proven able to fail
by a paired negative-control battery. No capability-token bump: the contract
remains `contract: review-journal/v0`, tightened in place while draft.

## Highlights

### PDR-0006: Crucible ships no consumer-linked code (accepted)

The repository's shipping charter, previously implicit, now written down and
citable. Rule 1 (linkage): crucible ships nothing that consuming code imports,
links, or depends on; it may ship standalone canonical tools whose sole purpose
is making its contracts falsifiable or materializable, and any such tool is
admitted by the corresponding conformance fixtures, never by fiat. Rule 2
(dependencies): any canonical tool crucible ships is standard-library-only —
an independent constraint, refused on principle rather than hygiene.

The record was amended in the same cycle with its Rule 2 referent: Rule 2a
names which tools the rule governs (canonical contract tooling and anything
held out for copying or adoption; repository-internal infrastructure is not a
charter matter), and Rule 2b defines the rule for tools that orchestrate other
programs — a declared, closed set of external commands, each justified as
subject-matter (a mechanism the contract itself names) or on a fixed baseline
list (POSIX `sh`, coreutils, `git`). Undeclared invocation is nonconformance,
and extending the baseline is an amendment, not a siting judgment.

### Running a Fierce-Collaboration Review (guide)

The run-book companion to the standard at
`docs/guides/running-a-fierce-collaboration-review.md`: executable steps from
declaring a review (claim, head anchors, roster, ceiling, gates, human merge
authority) through independent first passes, the finding register, remediation
and re-verification, to a claim-scoped disposition and downward export. A
redacted worked example from a real panel is threaded through every step, a
failure-modes table maps each known failure to the step that catches it, and
copy-paste templates cover the alignment-log header, gate table, disposition
table, finding register, and execution disclosure. The docs index gains a
Guides category, and the standard links the run-book as its non-normative
mechanics layer.

### `review-journal/v0` enforcement batch

The contract now enforces:

- **Classification floors, in-schema** — `access_tier ≥ sensitivity` per the
  published minimum-tier table, on every entry and on the manifest ceiling;
  half-`unknown` pairs fail closed in both directions.
- **Anchor label integrity** — `sha256:` and `git-tree:<object-format>:<oid>`
  forms are constrained so a label never claims an algorithm it does not use.
- **A mandatory, unambiguous participant join** — every roster seat declares
  its participant (with a `reasoner` that admits `not-exposed`); every event
  carries a required `agent.participant_ref`; duplicate roster seats and a
  ceiling setter that resolves to no roster participant are failures, never
  passes.
- **Prompt identity** — `role_prompt.digest` is required for approving agent
  review seats; the author and the human maintainer are outside that
  requirement by design, and the maintainer seat's participant must be human.
- **Finding lifecycle and claim-scoped gates** — machine-readable `lifecycle`
  (deferral requires an owner and closure trigger), `defect_class`, and
  `gate_scope` on gate events.
- **Author-does-not-approve** — at the seat level in-schema and at the person
  level through the participant join in a journal-set check, alongside the
  cross-stream ceiling and roster closure.

Every enforcement is demonstrated able to fail: 19 reject fixtures, each paired
with a baseline twin differing in exactly one field — an invariant the battery
asserts mechanically with a structural JSON diff that is itself self-tested
before anything is measured with it. Set-level fixtures are additionally
asserted schema-valid, so a set-level reject provably fails only at the set
gate. All controls run in `make check`.

### Records move with the mechanisms

PDR-0005's deferred-gap table records seven of its eight gaps closed with
evidence; `human-merge-authority` remains open as structural to the record — a
journal cannot prove who performed a merge, and the release says so rather than
implying otherwise. The standard moves 0.4.1 → 0.5.0 with its enforcement
disclosures updated to match: the variance-comparison claim moves from "join
not possible" to "join possible, conclusions not yet evidenced."

## Compatibility

`review-journal/v0` is draft and was tightened in place: journals emitted
against v0.1.24 that lack participants, `participant_ref`, `ceiling.set_by`,
or approving-seat prompt digests will not validate against v0.1.25 schemas.
The pass also adds enforceable constraints, not only new required fields — a
v0.1.24-era journal that violates the classification floors, labeled-anchor
forms, lifecycle conditionals, or the seat-level author-does-not-approve rule
now fails validation rather than passing on shape alone.
The event `agent.model` field is deprecated but retained; `agent.reasoner` is
its replacement. No other contract surface changes.
